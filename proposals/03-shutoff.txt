# Shutoff: why a single water-main break in Houston can dry out a whole neighborhood

> **One line:** When a Houston water main breaks, crews close the nearest valves. The City's own records mark **21,574 of those valves inoperable**, and **15,781 as "cannot locate"**. Shutoff simulates a break on any real main and shows the outage area on paper against the area in reality. It then picks which valves to repair first for the largest reduction in outages and water lost.

**Targets:** Best Overall ($1,000), Best Environment Hack ($500, under "resource use"), Best Pitch/Demo ($500)

**Houston news coverage of the inoperable-valve records:** none found. To our knowledge the 21,574 figure has not been reported publicly.

**The mechanism:** a break is isolated by closing the valves around it. If one of those valves is seized, buried or lost, crews must move out to the next valve. The shutoff spreads to more homes, takes longer, and loses more water while crews search.

## 2. What we build

1. **Water network graph** from 290k mains, with pipe endpoints as nodes (the City's data shares endpoint coordinates exactly). Valves are snapped onto pipes: in our sample 57% sit exactly on a pipe and 60% within 2 m.
2. **Valve segments.** Split the graph at valves into "segments", the smallest areas that can be isolated (Walski's segment concept). Compute this twice:
   - **on paper:** every valve works
   - **in reality:** valves marked `INOP` or `CANNOT_LOCATE` are treated as stuck open, so neighboring segments merge
3. **Break simulation.** Click any main. The tool returns the valves to close, the outage area (paper vs. reality), affected customers (544,011 City customer-account points), and an estimate of water lost (drain-down volume from pipe length and diameter, plus flow until shutoff).
4. **Expected impact per segment.** Break likelihood (material × age × length, from published break rates) × customers affected × gallons lost.
5. **Valve repair planner.** Given a budget of *N* valve repairs, choose the valves whose repair most reduces expected customer-outage-hours or gallons lost. The objective is set-based and greedy-friendly; state the approximation honestly.

## 3. 90-second demo

| Time | Judge sees | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 s | Houston map. Every inoperable valve is a red dot, and there are 21,574 of them. | "These are valves Houston's own records say don't work, or can't be found." |
| 15–40 s | **Judge clicks** any water main in their own neighborhood. It "breaks". The paper outage appears small and blue, then expands red to the real outage. | "On paper this break shuts off 40 homes. In reality, because two valves here are dead, it's 600." |
| 40–60 s | Side panel: customers affected, valves crews must close, estimated gallons lost, nearby care facilities | "And while crews hunt for a valve that isn't there, the water keeps running." |
| 60–80 s | **Judge drags** a "valves repaired" slider from 0 to 200. Citywide expected outage-hours and gallons lost fall, and the chosen valves light up. | "Repairing a valve costs far less than replacing a main. Here are the 200 that matter most." |
| 80–90 s | Close | "Houston replaced 6% of its pipes in a decade. This is what to fix tomorrow." |

## 4. Fit with Orion's judging criteria

| Criterion | How Shutoff scores |
|---|---|
| **Originality** | No hackathon project found (Devpost search for valve isolation, shutoff, criticality). **Commercial prior art exists:** Bentley WaterGEMS does segmentation and criticality, including inoperable valves; Autodesk InfoWater Pro has criticality in preview; Esri's Utility Network has isolation traces. Novel parts: built on a city's *public* records, uses the real inoperable-valve flags, ranks repairs, runs in a browser. |
| **Relevance to track (Environment)** | "Resource use": gallons lost per break and gallons saved per valve repaired must be in the product, not only in the pitch. This is the weakest fit of the two environment ideas. |
| **Impact** | Every Houstonian has seen a main break. Houston loses billions of gallons a year. A cheap, actionable fix. |
| **Demo** | Very strong: the judge breaks a pipe on their own street and watches the outage spread. |
| **Technical implementation** | Graph segmentation over 290k edges, valve snapping, a segment-merge model, a risk model, and repair optimization. **No ground-truth validation:** there are no public break or outage records to compare against. |

## 5. Business case

- **Buyer:** Houston Public Works, Houston Water (distribution operations, asset management). Secondary: any utility with GIS valve condition data; valve-exercising contractors.
- **Existing alternatives:**

  | Alternative | Gap |
  |---|---|
  | Bentley WaterGEMS / Autodesk InfoWater | Licensed desktop tools that need modelers; criticality is a feature, not a work plan |
  | Esri Utility Network isolation trace | Needs a migrated Utility Network; community reports problems filtering inoperable valves |
  | Valve-exercising programs (AWWA G200 practice) | Cycle through valves on a schedule rather than by impact |

- **Differentiation:** zero-install, works from the city's existing GIS export, prioritizes valve repairs by impact.
- **Beyond the hackathon:** the same tool for any city that publishes valve condition. Add work-order history to validate.

## 6. Technical design

```
City ArcGIS REST (mains, valves, customer accounts, hydrants) ─► ingest (Python) ─► GeoParquet
          │
  node pipes by endpoints; snap valves to nearest pipe (≤ X m), splitting edges
          │
  segment finding: remove valve nodes → connected components (paper)
                   keep INOP valves as pass-through → merged components (reality)
          │
  per segment: customers (point-in-buffer), pipe length/diameter → drain-down volume, break likelihood
          │
  repair planner: greedy over INOP valves by Δ expected impact
          │
  export: segment polygons/IDs + edge→segment map + valve list ─► React + MapLibre/deck.gl
```

- **Scope** the interactive demo to a few council districts, about 30–60k pipes. Citywide numbers are precomputed.
- Segmentation is linear-time connected components. Done offline it costs nothing.

No correlation gets reported honestly. Current water-leak 311 data is not public (the archive service is stopped).

## 8. Claims we will and won't make

- **Will:** "The City's records mark 21,574 valves inoperable. Under our model, the average break's outage grows by X%." Label it as a model.
- **Won't:** claim the valve flags are current. They are the City's records; show "condition date" ranges. Won't claim validation against real outages, because none is possible with public data.

| "Bentley already does this" | Name it. Pitch public data, real inoperable flags, repair ranking and no license. |
